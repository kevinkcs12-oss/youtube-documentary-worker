**PASS FOR HUMAN EDITORIAL REVIEW. NOT A VOICE SELECTION. NOT A RELEASE MASTER.**

## Bias controls

- Identical excerpt intent and scoring criteria are shown for all candidates.
- Visual treatment is identical except for the anonymous candidate label.
- Candidate order uses a Latin-square rotation: `ABCD`, `BCDA`, `CDAB`, `DABC`.
- Every candidate occupies first, second, third and fourth position exactly once.
- Two-second excerpt slates and one-second post-candidate resets separate judgments.

## Source integrity

The source MP3s are the previously sealed, rights-verified audition files. Excerpts were trimmed only at detected inter-excerpt silence boundaries. No time-stretch, pitch shift, re-synthesis, rewriting, or automatic ranking was applied. The comparison reel adds one AAC encode for review convenience; the sealed source pack remains the fidelity authority.

## Red-team findings

- The rotation reduces systematic primacy and recency bias but cannot eliminate listener fatigue or contrast effects.
- Anonymous letters may still acquire preferences across excerpts; scores should be entered immediately after each excerpt rather than reconstructed from memory.
- Loudness was intentionally not normalized candidate-by-candidate because that could change perceived dynamics; reviewers must not treat level alone as performance quality.
- The reel is sufficient for a human decision, but no candidate may be promoted without a completed scorecard and explicit authorization.

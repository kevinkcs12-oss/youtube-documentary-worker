## Evidence families F1–F7

### F1 — Historical object-colour sample

- **Source:** Cath Sleeman, Creative Industries Policy & Evidence Centre, “A little greyer and a bit more rectangular?” (2021; page updated 2025).
- **Link:** https://pec.ac.uk/blog_entries/a-little-greyer/
- **Used for:** a substantial rise in grey and falls in brown/yellow within photographs of objects from the Science Museum Group collection; changing materials are presented as a possible contributor.
- **Boundary:** the photographed objects are a sample from a non-random museum collection. This is not a global estimate of everything people owned.

### F2 — New-vehicle colour distribution

- **Source:** Axalta, “2025 Global Automotive Color Popularity Report” (released 16 December 2025).
- **Link:** https://ir.axalta.com/news/press-releases/detail/673/axalta-releases-2025-global-automotive-color-popularity-report
- **Used for:** global white 29%, black 23%, grey 22%, silver 7%; Europe grey 26%. White, black and grey total 74%; adding silver gives 81%.
- **Boundary:** Axalta reports production-market colour shares. It does not establish one causal explanation for those choices.

### F3 — Processing fluency and aesthetic response

- **Source:** Reber, Schwarz & Winkielman (2004), “Processing Fluency and Aesthetic Pleasure: Is Beauty in the Perceiver’s Processing Experience?” *Personality and Social Psychology Review*, 8(4), 364–382.
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1207/S15327957PSPR0804_3
- **Used for:** the proposed relationship between fluent processing and positive aesthetic response, including effects associated with contrast, repetition, symmetry and prototypicality.
- **Boundary:** a review and theoretical account, not a universal rule that simple or familiar design is objectively beautiful.

### F4 — Familiar interaction conventions

- **Source:** Jakob Nielsen, Nielsen Norman Group, “End of Web Design” (22 July 2000).
- **Link:** https://www.nngroup.com/articles/end-of-web-design/
- **Used for:** Jakob’s Law—the observation that users spend most of their time on other sites and therefore expect familiar interaction patterns.
- **Boundary:** this supports transfer of learning and reduced interaction friction. It does not require a single visual aesthetic.

### F5 — Responsive identity systems

- **Source:** Mihajlović, Gajić, Stanković & Tair (2016), “The Importance of Responsive Logo Design Across a Wide Range of Devices on the Web,” Sinteza 2016, pp. 50–55.
- **Link:** https://portal.sinteza.singidunum.ac.rs/paper/396
- **DOI:** https://doi.org/10.15308/Sinteza-2016-50-55
- **Used for:** responsive logo variants can remove unnecessary detail to remain clear and readable across screen sizes.
- **Boundary:** this does not prove that mobile devices caused minimalist rebranding or that every responsive identity must look alike.

### F6 — Architecture and repeatable systems

- **Source:** McDonald’s corporate history.
- **Link:** https://corporate.mcdonalds.com/corpmcd/our-company/who-we-are/our-history.html
- **Used for:** the attention-catching 1953 Red and White design; replacement by the Mansard Roof design in the late 1960s; introduction of the Mansard design in 1969; later use of existing architecture and unique features.
- **Boundary:** a company-authored chronology illustrating one system’s evolution, not evidence that all chains or modern locations became uniform grey boxes.

### F7 — Recommendation trade-offs

- **Source:** Mehrotra et al., Spotify Research, “Algorithmic Balancing of Familiarity, Similarity, & Discovery in Music Recommendations” (CIKM; 5 November 2021).
- **Link:** https://research.atspotify.com/publications/algorithmic-balancing-of-familiarity-similarity-discovery-in-music-recommendations
- **Used for:** the explicit trade-off between familiarity, similarity and discovery in music recommendation.
- **Boundary:** Spotify’s research concerns music recommendation. The documentary’s visual-convergence loop is an explanatory model, not a Spotify finding.

## Named counterexamples — existence, not prevalence

- **Material Design 3 — colour:** https://m3.material.io/styles/color/overview
- **IBM Carbon Design System — colour:** https://carbondesignsystem.com/elements/color/overview/
- **Porsche Paint to Sample:** https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/products/porsche-911-gt3-paint-to-sample-porsche-exclusive-manufaktur-27832.html

These pages establish that distinct usable design systems and bespoke colour choices exist. They do not measure how common, successful or economically superior those exceptions are.

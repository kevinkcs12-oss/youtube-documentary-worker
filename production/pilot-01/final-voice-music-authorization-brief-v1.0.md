## 1. Executive decision

The animatic timing is frozen as the current picture-lock candidate. The next irreversible-quality dependency is the final narration performance; music must support that performance rather than determine its pace.

Recommended sequence:

1. Authorize a no-cost, internal voice audition only.
2. Compare blind-labelled voice tests against the same locked audition reel and scorecard.
3. Select a route only after rights, provenance, commercial-use terms, and total cost are documented.
4. Record or generate final narration sequence by sequence against v1.2f.
5. Reconfirm timing before acquiring or licensing music.
6. Build music and sound design around the approved narration, then run full rights, evidence, caption, loudness, and decode QA.

This order prevents music spend from anchoring the film to a voice performance that has not yet passed cadence and meaning tests.

## 2. Non-negotiable voice brief

### Editorial character

- English-language premium documentary voice: calm, observant, intelligent, and human.
- Authority comes from precision and restraint, not theatrical certainty.
- The opening should feel curious rather than ominous.
- Quantitative passages must remain intelligible without sounding like an advertisement or list recital.
- Methodological limits must receive equal clarity and conviction as headline findings.
- Counterexamples should sound like genuine falsification, not a token disclaimer.
- The conclusion should become warmer and more reflective without turning inspirational or sentimental.

### Prohibited performance tendencies

- Conspiracy-trailer intensity, breathy “secret revealed” delivery, or manufactured urgency.
- Generic synthetic cadence, repeated sentence-final drops, excessive dramatic pauses, or over-polished commercial warmth.
- Compressing qualifications, source limits, percentages, or the Spotify boundary.
- Adding, deleting, paraphrasing, or editorializing the locked narration.
- Time-stretching a finished voice to rescue a failed performance.

### Timing and cadence

- Preserve the v1.2f runtime and all existing speech boundaries.
- Target natural delivery around 138–146 words per minute, with line-level variation for numbers and conclusions.
- Replacement A must remain comfortable inside 14 seconds; Replacement B inside 54 seconds.
- No finished-voice acceleration. A line that does not fit naturally is re-performed once; if it still fails, timing reopens only at that line after evidence-boundary review.
- Preserve approximately two seconds of transition breath at the two intentionally retained rests.

### Recording/delivery specification

- 48 kHz, 24-bit, mono WAV; one clean take per sequence plus selected pickups.
- Raw peaks at or below -6 dBFS; no clipping, hard limiting, aggressive denoising, reverb, music, or baked mastering.
- Stable microphone distance and room sound across sessions.
- Supply 30 seconds of room tone and a pronunciation pickup file.
- File naming: `P01_S##_TAKE##_voice-route.wav`; pickups include cue or sentence identifier.

## 3. Locked audition reel

Use one blind-labelled reel for every route. Do not tune the script to favor a particular voice.

### A — Hook and scope

> Walk through almost any new part of a city and something feels strangely familiar. The cars arrive in the same narrow palette. The cafés share pale wood, black metal, and green. Logos use clean letters. New apartment blocks repeat grids of glass, concrete, and muted cladding. It can feel as if all of it came from one invisible catalog. But the world does not literally look the same.

Tests curiosity, list rhythm, tonal restraint, and the pivot away from universality.

### B — Numbers and evidence boundary

> In 2025: white 29%, black 23%, gray 22%, and silver 7%. White, black, and gray together: 74%. Add silver, and the grayscale share reaches 81%. In Europe, gray alone led at 26%. That proves dominance. It does not prove why. Several forces may contribute. The report does not isolate or rank them.

Tests number clarity, pacing, emphasis discipline, and whether the causal limit lands as strongly as the statistic.

### C — Explanatory model boundary

> Spotify researchers describe balancing familiarity and similarity against discovery. The platform does not simply maximize sameness; it manages competing objectives. Algorithms did not invent visual convergence. They can accelerate a loop that already exists. This loop is our model, not Spotify’s finding.

Tests named-source pronunciation, conceptual clarity, and protection against inflated causality.

### D — Final landing

> The world does not have to become identical for difference to become harder to defend. And perhaps that is the hidden cost of frictionless design. We notice fewer bad surprises—but also fewer good ones. Sameness is rarely imposed all at once. It accumulates, one reasonable decision at a time. And difference returns the same way: one defensible exception at a time.

Tests emotional range, silence discipline, and a reflective ending without slogan delivery.

## 4. Voice evaluation and failure gates

Score each route from 1–5 against the attached scorecard. Weighted pass threshold: 80/100.

Hard failure regardless of score:

- Any word, number, source, or qualification becomes unclear or semantically distorted.
- Replacement A or B requires finished-voice acceleration or clipped breaths.
- The delivery suggests universal causality, conspiracy, or certainty beyond the script.
- Commercial-use rights, performer consent, model provenance, or reuse terms are ambiguous.
- Audible clipping, unstable room tone, material synthetic artifacts, or fatigue across a ten-minute extrapolation.

Tie-break order: editorial truth, sustained listenability, timing fit, then cost. Novelty never outranks intelligibility or rights clarity.

## 5. Voice routes

### Free / no-purchase routes

**F1 — Rights-verified local or open-source synthetic audition**  
Use only for internal comparison until both engine and voice/model commercial-use terms are captured. Highest operational speed; highest risk of generic cadence and unclear model provenance. It cannot become the release voice on the basis of “free” alone.

**F2 — Self-recorded or already-authorized human audition**  
No provider fee and strongest provenance if the performer owns the recording and supplies explicit commercial consent. Quality depends on English delivery, room, microphone, and performance consistency. No third party is contacted under the present authorization.

**F3 — Temporary scratch voice retained**  
Zero acquisition cost but rejected as a release default. The current synthetic track is a timing instrument and is not evidence of final-listening quality or release rights.

### Paid routes — researchable, not authorized for commitment

**P1 — Professional human narrator**  
Best ceiling for nuance, methodological restraint, and ten-minute listenability. Requires performer selection, commercial usage scope, revision terms, payment, and consent documentation. No outreach or booking before explicit authorization.

**P2 — Commercial synthetic voice service**  
Fast pickup workflow and reproducibility, but requires provider, plan, model/voice rights, training-data or consent assurances where available, commercial-use scope, renewal risk, and total-cost review. No paid generation, subscription, or credit purchase before explicit authorization.

**P3 — Bespoke voice clone**  
Not recommended for the pilot unless the consenting voice owner and reuse boundaries are exceptionally clear. Consent, identity, revocation, storage, and impersonation risks exceed the pilot’s current need.

## 6. Music and sound brief

### Narrative function

Music should create continuity and controlled momentum, never serve as factual evidence or imply certainty. Use restraint: the visual argument is already information-dense.

Proposed four-part architecture:

1. **00:00–01:04 — Familiarity with unease:** sparse pulse or texture; curious, not sinister.
2. **01:04–03:30 — Evidence and friction:** minimal neutral bed; leave space around percentages and methodological cautions.
3. **03:30–08:36 — Systems and feedback:** quiet mechanical continuity with subtle evolution; avoid “technology reveal” clichés and any data-like sonic implication.
4. **08:36–09:57.760 — Answer and return:** warmer harmonic opening, still unresolved enough to protect the qualified conclusion; finish under the final phrase rather than announcing it.

Silence remains an editorial tool. Music does not need to run continuously. Protect pauses around “It does not prove why,” “This loop is our model, not Spotify’s finding,” the counterexample reversals, and the final sentence.

### Free / no-purchase routes

**M1 — No music; narration plus original, minimal sound design**  
Strongest rights simplicity and useful control version. Requires only assets whose authorship and provenance are documented.

**M2 — Rights-verified free library tracks**  
Potentially viable only after per-track commercial-use terms, attribution obligations, content-ID behavior, version, download date, source URL, and file checksum are recorded. “Royalty-free” is not treated as proof of unrestricted use.

### Paid routes — researchable, not authorized for commitment

**M3 — Licensed production-music library**  
Efficient if the exact licence covers monetized online documentary use, territory, duration, channel count, edits, and continuing availability after cancellation.

**M4 — Original commissioned score**  
Highest distinctiveness and edit fit, but requires outreach, fee, rights transfer or licence scope, revisions, stems, credits, and delivery schedule. Not justified until the final voice passes.

### Music rejection gates

- Rights or content-ID outcome is ambiguous.
- The bed masks narration, percentages, source names, or evidence limits.
- The cue creates conspiracy, luxury-advertising, motivational, or generic “AI documentary” tone.
- A build, hit, or drop implies causality not present in the narration.
- The edit requires shortening evidence cards or moving locked speech.
- The licence depends on an undisclosed renewal, auto-charge, or incompatible publication scope.

## 7. Mix and delivery targets

- Dialogue is the mix anchor; preserve intelligibility on phone speakers and at low listening levels.
- Final programme target: -16 to -14 LUFS integrated, 5–10 LU loudness range, true peak at or below -1 dBTP.
- Music under narration is adjusted by intelligibility, not one fixed number; begin roughly 12–18 dB below narration and automate locally.
- No audible pumping from ducking, no masking of consonants, and no low-frequency buildup under evidence passages.
- Deliver dialogue, music, sound effects, and full-mix stems at 48 kHz / 24-bit, plus the encoded review master.
- QA on studio headphones, ordinary laptop, phone speaker, and low-volume mono fold-down.

These are production targets, not platform guarantees; the retained master is accepted only after measured and perceptual QA.

## 8. Rights and provenance ledger required before release

For every retained voice, cue, ambience, and sound effect record:

- asset title and unique local name;
- creator/performer/provider;
- original source URL or contract reference;
- acquisition/download date;
- exact licence text or consent record and captured version/date;
- commercial/monetized online use, editing, territory, duration, attribution, and content-ID status;
- cost, currency, renewal status, and invoice/reference if applicable;
- SHA-256 of the retained source and rendered derivative;
- approved use window in the film;
- reviewer and final disposition.

Missing provenance means exclusion, not provisional picture lock.

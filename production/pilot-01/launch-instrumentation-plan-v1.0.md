# Pilot 01 — Launch Instrumentation Plan v1.0

Timing authority: `v1.2f-pacing-trim`, 09:57.760 / 14,944 frames

## Decision

Prepare one native YouTube title-and-thumbnail experiment with three materially distinct, claim-safe variants. Use YouTube's own A/B result as the packaging decision authority because the platform chooses the option with the highest watch time, not raw click-through rate alone. Keep the test unchanged until YouTube completes it or until a hard factual, rights, or expectation-mismatch defect requires withdrawal.

This plan authorizes no upload, publication, external promotion, purchase, contact, or channel-setting change.

## Locked experiment cells

| Cell | Title | Thumbnail proposition | Intended question |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — Curiosity | Why Does Everything Look the Same Now? | WHY SO SAME? + repeated objects | Does the canonical broad question earn qualified curiosity? |
| B — Concrete scope | Why Cars, Apps and Buildings Started Looking Alike | CARS / APPS / BUILDINGS + SAME? | Does naming three domains improve expectation match? |
| C — Mechanism | How Safe Choices Push Design Toward Sameness | THE SAFE CHOICE LOOP | Does the documentary's bounded mechanism attract deeper viewing? |

Do not combine parts across cells during the first test. YouTube permits up to three title/thumbnail combinations, and manually changing either element stops an active test.

## Measurement hierarchy

1. **Native A/B winner by watch time** — primary packaging outcome.
2. **First-30-second retention** — expectation-match diagnostic, available after YouTube processes retention data.
3. **Average view duration and average percentage viewed** — whole-film delivery.
4. **Timestamped retention shape** — flat segments, gradual decline, spikes and dips, interpreted against the frozen chapter map.
5. **Impressions and CTR by traffic source** — reach diagnostic, never a standalone creative verdict.
6. **Qualified actions** — subscribers gained from the video and substantive comments, treated as secondary signals rather than optimization targets.

## Read schedule and minimum-evidence policy

| Read | Permitted use | Prohibited reaction |
|---|---|---|
| 6 hours | Confirm processing, test status, gross technical faults | No CTR or retention verdict |
| 24 hours | Record reach and traffic-source mix; note obvious expectation mismatch | No cosmetic change on small fluctuations |
| 72 hours | First structured retention review if processed | No causal claim from one dip |
| 7 days | Compare watch-time test state, chapters and traffic sources | No cross-source CTR averaging |
| 14 days or native test completion | Lock the winning package or record “no clear winner” | No manual winner invented when YouTube reports none |

Operational evidence tiers are internal safeguards, not YouTube benchmarks:

- fewer than 1,000 registered impressions: observation only, except a hard defect;
- 1,000–4,999 impressions: diagnose large expectation mismatches, but avoid marginal packaging conclusions;
- 5,000 or more impressions: packaging interpretation is permitted, with the native A/B result still controlling.

| Time | Editorial function | What failure would mean |
|---:|---|---|
| 00:30 | Hook and scope | Likely title/thumbnail promise mismatch or weak opening clarity |
| 01:04 | Impression → evidence | Evidence handoff loses viewers or arrives too slowly |
| 02:22 | Processing fluency | Quantitative module is too dense or the mechanism pivot is unclear |
| 03:30.640 | Interfaces and identities | First pacing trim or chapter reset feels abrupt |
| 04:45.800 | Architecture and scale | Second pacing trim or domain change feels abrupt |
| 06:04.800 | Feedback loop | Accumulated explanatory fatigue before synthesis |
| 07:19.800 | Falsification | Counterexamples arrive too late or feel disconnected |
| 08:35.800 | Conclusion | Final synthesis loses viewers after the reversal |
| 09:57.760 | End | Completion baseline; no universal target assumed |

For every apparent dip, inspect a 20-second window before and after it, the narration line, on-screen evidence load, traffic-source mix and sample size. A dip is a diagnostic location, not proof of its cause.

## Decision rules

- **Packaging pass:** retain the native winner only if no claim, rights or expectation-match defect is found.
- **No clear winner:** keep Cell A, the canonical title, unless retention/comment evidence identifies a specific mismatch.
- **High CTR / weak retention:** suspect packaging overpromise before cutting evidence.
- **Low CTR / strong retention:** packaging is the first revision surface; do not reopen picture timing.
- **Shared dip across all cells:** investigate the film at that timestamp, not the package.
- **Cell-specific retention difference:** treat as a packaging-audience interaction; wait for adequate exposure and traffic-source context.
- **Timing reopen gate:** only a repeatable P0 intelligibility, evidence, or transition failure may reopen v1.2f. Cosmetic preference is insufficient.

## Metadata and chapter control

- The description must state that the film examines converging incentives through bounded cases; it does not claim every design is identical or that one actor coordinates the outcome.
- Chapters must use the exact v1.2f timing map supplied in `Pilot_01_Description_Chapters_v1.0.txt`.
- Evidence sources belong in the description or pinned reference note only after link and rights review.
- Do not add brands, logos, copied interfaces, fabricated statistics, urgency language, or conspiracy framing to titles or thumbnails.

## Red-team findings

Rejected before retention:

1. A CTR-first decision rule — rejected because CTR varies by audience and traffic source, and YouTube's native experiment selects on watch time.
2. Immediate 6-hour optimization — rejected because audience-retention processing typically takes 1–2 days and early CTR can reflect a narrow audience.
3. A title claiming “The Algorithm Made Everything Look the Same” — rejected as unsupported monocausal framing.
4. A thumbnail using recognizable brand logos — rejected on rights, bias and evidentiary-boundary grounds.
5. Fixed universal “good CTR” and retention targets — rejected because channel size, audience and traffic source materially change the baseline.

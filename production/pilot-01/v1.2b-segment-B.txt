One developer copies a layout that sold quickly. One designer chooses a familiar typeface. One café recreates a photogenic interior. One platform recommends work similar to what held attention.

No single choice transforms culture. Each becomes evidence for the next.

Spotify researchers describe balancing familiarity and similarity against discovery. The platform does not simply maximize sameness; it manages competing objectives.

Algorithms did not invent visual convergence. They can accelerate a loop that already exists: what is familiar is easier to recognize; what is easier to recognize is easier to choose; what is chosen becomes easier to measure; and what is measured becomes easier to imitate.

This loop is our explanatory model, not Spotify's finding. It is not a conspiracy, but a system in which nobody wants to be the expensive exception.
